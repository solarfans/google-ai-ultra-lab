Google just launched Tasks in Gemini, and it turns AI from a chatbot into a background assistant that actually finishes the job.
In Chat mode, you ask a question and wait. Flip to Task mode, hand over a real job, and walk away while Gemini works in the cloud.
Here is how people actually use it. First, a daily morning briefing. Type, every morning at seven a.m., scan the biggest AI launches and send me a one-page summary.
Second, multi-step web research. Give it a topic, and you can watch Gemini think, search Google, visit live websites, and return a clean table or editable Canvas report.
Third, recurring monitors. Schedule weekly website or price checks, or connect Gmail and Drive so Gemini flags important emails and drafts replies before Monday morning.
And it never goes rogue. Before touching your Google Drive, sending an email, or signing into a site, Gemini pauses and knocks until you click Connect.
Google rolled out a free tier in six countries, the U.S., Canada, Japan, South Korea, Australia, and India, with ten daily schedules. Pro and Ultra unlock fifty schedules, fifteen parallel tasks, and global access.
Two quick tips before you start. First, Tasks share a rolling five-hour compute budget, so keep each prompt tightly scoped instead of dumping ten jobs into one. Second, save repeat workflows as slash Skills so you never retype instructions.
To try your first task today, open Gemini, flip to Task, describe one clear job, put the timing first, and review the first run. Follow for more practical AI breakdowns.
